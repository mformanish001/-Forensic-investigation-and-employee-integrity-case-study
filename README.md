# Forensic-investigation-and-employee-integrity-case-study

A fictional forensic investigation and employee integrity case study
Prepared by: Manish Kumar
Case reference: Silverline – Marketing Procurement Integrity Review
Complaint received: 26 October 2026
Project type: Independent portfolio simulation  
Disclaimer: This project is entirely fictional and created for learning and portfolio demonstration. The companies, employees, allegations and evidence are simulated. It is not an EY engagement, an actual investigation, or a finding of misconduct.

Project overview
Silverline Consumer Products Ltd. receives an anonymous ethics-hotline complaint concerning a marketing procurement contract awarded to BlueArc Media Solutions Pvt Ltd. The complaint alleges preferential vendor selection, billing for incomplete campaign work, and a possible undisclosed relationship between the supplier and the marketing procurement lead, Neha Malhotra.

The project follows a focused four-step forensic review: complaint assessment, source-document examination, preliminary employee integrity and wealth assessment, and reporting of findings and recommendations.

Investigation workflow
1. Complaint deconstruction
Review the anonymous allegation, identify relevant parties, separate claims into investigative issues, and define evidence-gathering steps.

Deliverable: 01_Complaint_Deconstruction_Note.docx
2. Document discrepancy analysis
Compare the bid extract, purchase order, supplier invoice, delivery acceptance record and credit note. Reconcile billed amounts and flag missing acceptance evidence.

Deliverable: 02_Document_Discrepancy_Analysis.docx
3. Employee background and wealth analysis
Assess limited simulated indicators such as a vehicle purchase, lease commitments and an unidentified financial credit. Distinguish potential red flags from established facts and respect authorization, privacy and the employee's opportunity to respond.

Deliverable: 03_Employee_Background_and_Wealth_Analysis.docx
4. Final investigation report
Summarize evidence, preliminary findings, limitations and practical control recommendations. Avoid treating allegations as proven misconduct.

Deliverable: 04_Final_Investigation_Report.docx

Key observations
- Supplier selection: BlueArc's ₹48 lakh bid exceeded a competing ₹42 lakh bid; the available scorecard does not document a sufficient selection rationale.
- Service delivery: Final acceptance of the ₹12 lakh video-production item is pending. After a ₹4 lakh credit note, the remaining ₹8 lakh requires verification.
- Employee review: Lifestyle and financial indicators merit proportionate clarification; legitimate financing and household sources have not been ruled out.
- Conflict of interest: The alleged supplier–employee relationship is not substantiated by the provided records.
Recommended actions
1. Obtain complete tender scoring, approvals and the relevant delegation-of-authority rules.
2. Verify final campaign deliverables and acceptance evidence before resolving the disputed billing amount.
3. Confirm that the credit note was recorded and reconcile the actual payment status.
4. Review employee declarations and financial explanations only through authorized, privacy-compliant procedures.
5. Improve documentation of vendor-selection decisions and independent verification of service completion.
Skills demonstrated
Forensic investigation: complaint triage, red-flag identification, evidence review, discrepancy analysis, professional skepticism and preliminary reporting.
Risk and compliance: procurement controls, conflict-of-interest assessment, employee integrity screening and documentation of investigative limitations.
Business communication: structured Word reports and an executive PowerPoint summary.
